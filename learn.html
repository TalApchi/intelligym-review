Training tips

Learn to read the game

Five skills sit behind what coaches call game intelligence. Here is what each one means in a game, and habits that help.

The five skills

Skill 1

Scanning

Looking around before the ball arrives.

Quick looks over the shoulder and across the field, taken while the ball is on its way, so you already know where teammates, opponents and space are when it reaches you.

On the field

  • Check both shoulders before you receive.
  • Look again just before the pass is played: the picture changes.

Tested in: Opponents vanish

Skill 2

Awareness

Keeping the whole picture in mind.

Holding on to where several players are and how they are moving, not only the player nearest the ball, and noticing when something changes.

On the field

  • Know where your nearest marker is without staring at them.
  • Keep track of runs off the ball, not just the ball.

Tested in: Spot the change, Tracking runners

Skill 3

Anticipation

Seeing what is about to happen.

Reading body shape, runs and the position of the ball to predict the next action, so you move a moment earlier than the play.

On the field

  • Watch the passer's hips and standing foot.
  • Ask yourself where the ball is likely to go next, then move.

Tested in: Through ball

Skill 4

Decision-making

Choosing the best option in the time you have.

Weighing passing, dribbling and shooting options against the risk of losing the ball, then committing. Often there is more than one good answer.

On the field

  • Have a first and a second option before you receive.
  • A safe pass back can be the right call; so can a dribble into space.

Tested in: Tactical choice, Opponents vanish

Skill 5

Timing

Doing it at the right moment.

Releasing a pass, making a run or stepping in at the moment it works: too early and the chance is not there yet, too late and it has gone.

On the field

  • Pass into a run when the runner is level with the last defender, not after.
  • Delay your run until the passer can see you.

Tested in: Through ball

Tips for players, coaches and parents

Players

Look before the ball arrives

In every passing drill, check over both shoulders before the ball reaches you. At first, count your looks out loud; it soon becomes a habit you do without thinking.

Players · Coaches

Play small-sided games

3v3 and 4v4 games give every player more touches and many more decisions than a full match, with less time on the ball.

Players · Coaches

Limit the touches

One- or two-touch rules force players to decide before the ball arrives, which is where scanning pays off.

Coaches · Parents

Say what you saw

After a move or a clip, ask the player what they saw and what else was on. Hearing their reasoning tells a coach more than the outcome does.

Players

Always have a second option

Before receiving, pick a first and a second choice. If the first is closed when the ball arrives, the second is already in your head.

Coaches · Parents

Praise the decision, not only the result

A good pass that a teammate misses was still a good read. Praising the decision keeps players willing to try the right thing.
